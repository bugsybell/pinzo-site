Privacy policy

Last updated 18 November 2026

The short version. Pinzo has no ads, analytics or trackers, and you don't need an account. Your rounds, photos, notes and profile are stored on your iPhone and in your own private iCloud, which Pinzo Golf Ltd cannot access. If you choose to use Friends, we keep a copy of your passport's basic facts (your name, home town, handicap and where and when you played) so the friends you invite can see it. Photos, notes and your home location are never shared. We also receive information if you email us.

Who we are

Pinzo Golf Ltd is the data controller for the limited information described below. For anything about privacy, email privacy@pinzo.golf.

What the app stores, and where

Your passport

The rounds you log (course, date, and optionally score, notes and the virtual course for indoor rounds), photos you attach, your bucket list and any venues you add are stored on your device. If iCloud is turned on for Pinzo, Apple syncs them to your private iCloud database so they appear on your other devices. This data is encrypted by Apple and is only accessible with your Apple Account; Pinzo Golf Ltd has no access to it and no copy of it.

Your profile

Your name, optional handicap index and home area are stored on your device and in your iCloud key-value storage, in the same private way.

Location

If you choose “Use my location”, Pinzo asks iOS for your approximate location once, to suggest courses near you. We round it to roughly one kilometre and keep it as your home area in your profile. You can instead type a town, or change or clear it at any time in Settings. Pinzo never tracks your location in the background.

Photos

Pinzo only sees the photos you pick with the iOS photo picker, for a round, its scorecard or your profile picture. If you scan a scorecard, Pinzo uses the camera only while the scanner is open. Photos and scorecards stay on your device and in your iCloud and are never shared with friends; location and camera details are removed when you add them.

Notifications

If you turn them on, Pinzo creates reminders on your iPhone: a nudge to log a round after Apple Health records a golf workout, occasional “on this day” memories, and alerts when you collect a stamp. They're scheduled by your phone; we don't run a notification server or receive any information from them.

Apple Health

If you choose to import rounds from Apple Health, Pinzo asks for read-only access to your workouts and workout routes. It reads golf workouts on your iPhone, uses a route's starting point to suggest the course, and adds only the rounds you select. If you turn on golf reminders, iOS lets Pinzo notice new golf workouts in the background so it can remind you to log them. Health data is never uploaded, shared or used for anything else, and Pinzo doesn't write to Apple Health. You can turn access off in Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices.

Friends (optional)

Friends lets you and the people you invite see where each other has played. It's off until you choose to use it, and you sign in with Apple to start. We don't ask Apple for your name or email.

How friends are added

There's no search, no public profile and no list of users. You add a friend by sending them a private invite link, which works once and expires after seven days. When they accept, you become friends with each other. You can remove or block a friend at any time, and they aren't told.

What your friends can see

  • your first and last name, home town and, if you've added them, your handicap index and favourite course;
  • each round's course, country and date, and so your stamps, milestones and the places you've played;
  • a round's score, only if you turn on Show score to friends for that round;
  • which of your friends you played a round with, if you tag them (they can then add that round to their own passport);
  • a notification when you log a new round, if they've turned those on.

Your photos, scorecards, notes, profile picture, bio, bucket list, home location, the names you type for people not on Pinzo and Apple Health data are never shared or uploaded.

What we store

To make this work we store: an anonymous account identifier from Sign in with Apple; the profile details and round facts listed above, including which friends you tagged; who you're friends with; invites you've created; anyone you've blocked; reports you make; and, if you allow friend notifications, a push token for your device. This is held by our database provider, Supabase (servers in Ireland), under a data processing agreement. Notifications are delivered by Apple's push notification service.

Our legal basis is providing the service you asked for. We keep this data until you leave Friends. Reports are kept for up to a year so we can act on them.

Leaving

Go to Settings → Leave Friends and delete account. This deletes your account, your shared passport, friendships, invites and push token from our servers straight away and disconnects Sign in with Apple. Your own passport isn't affected; it stays on your iPhone and in your iCloud.

Reports and safety

If you report someone, we see the report, the reason you give and the reported person's shared profile and rounds, and use them only to investigate. We may remove accounts that break our terms.

What we receive

Apart from Friends (above), nothing — unless you contact us. If you email support, we receive your email address and whatever you write, and use it only to reply. We keep support emails for as long as needed to help you, and delete them on request. Our email is provided by Google Workspace.

We don't use analytics, advertising or tracking tools, and we don't sell or share personal information. If you've opted in to share diagnostics with app developers in iOS settings, Apple may provide us with anonymous crash reports.

Services the app connects to

To show course information and images, the app makes requests to these services. They see your device's IP address, as with any website you visit, but receive nothing from your passport.

  • Apple Maps — maps and aerial views of courses (Apple's privacy policy applies).
  • Wikimedia Commons — openly licensed course photos (Wikimedia Foundation's privacy policy applies).
  • pinzo.golf — occasional course-list updates, hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub's privacy statement applies).
  • Supabase — only if you use Friends, to store your account and shared passport as described above.
  • Apple Push Notification service — only if you allow friend notifications.

If you share a passport or round card, you choose where it goes using the iOS share sheet; that service's own policy then applies.

Deleting your data

In the app, go to Profile → Settings → Delete all my data. This removes every round, photo, note, saved course, added venue and your profile from your device and your iCloud, and the rounds your friends can see. To remove your Friends account too, use Leave Friends and delete account. You can also delete the app, and remove its iCloud data in iOS Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Storage.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the UK GDPR or EU GDPR), you have rights to access, correct, delete, restrict or object to the processing of your personal data, and to data portability. Because your passport is held in your own iCloud, you can exercise most of these directly in the app. For anything else, email privacy@pinzo.golf. You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority — in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk), or the authority where you live in the EU.

Where we process the contents of a support email, our legal basis is our legitimate interest in answering you.

Children

Pinzo is a general-audience golf app, but Friends is only for people aged 16 and over, who confirm their age when they start. Friends can only see each other's golf facts; there's no messaging, no photos and no way for strangers to find anyone. If you believe someone under 16 is using Friends, email privacy@pinzo.golf and we'll delete their account.

This website

pinzo.golf uses no cookies, analytics or trackers. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, which may log visitors' IP addresses for security.

Changes

If we change how Pinzo handles information, we'll update this policy and explain the change in the app before it applies to you.
